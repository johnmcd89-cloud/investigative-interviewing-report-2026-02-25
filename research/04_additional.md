## Investigative Interviewing: Additional Priority Topics for Agencies
Practical recommendations below are **synthesized inferences** from the cited sources (UN, government guidance, and peer-reviewed research).

### 1) Trauma-informed interviewing
**Practical recommendations**
- Adopt an agency-wide trauma-informed framework: prioritize physical/psychological safety, trust, collaboration, and avoiding re-traumatization.
- Train interviewers not to equate fragmented memory, delayed disclosure, flat affect, or inconsistent sequencing with deception.
- Use phased interviews (rapport, free narrative, clarification, closure), with breaks and support options.
- Build multidisciplinary pathways (investigator + advocate/services) for high-trauma victim populations.

**Source URLs**
- https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/trauma-violence/trauma-informed-approaches-programs  
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/achieving-best-evidence-in-criminal-proceedings  
- https://nij.ojp.gov/topics/articles/practical-guide-interviewing-potential-human-trafficking-victims  

### 2) Child and vulnerable witness protocols
**Practical recommendations**
- Require structured protocols (e.g., NICHD-informed) instead of ad hoc interviewing.
- Start with ground rules and narrative practice; prioritize open prompts over focused/leading prompts.
- Use specialist interviewers for children and other high-vulnerability groups.
- Minimize duplicate interviews; electronically record first quality interview for evidential use/review.

### 3) Interpreter-mediated interviews
**Practical recommendations**
- Use qualified independent interpreters by default (and document any exception).
- Pre-brief interpreter on role boundaries (verbatim meaning transfer, no coaching, no side conversations), confidentiality, impartiality.
- Confirm comprehension early and repeatedly; pause if communication quality drops.
- Record interpreter identity, language/dialect, and mode (in-person/remote) in interview records.

### 4) Contamination and suggestion risk
**Practical recommendations**
- Require “free account first,” then clarification; restrict suggestive/leading prompts to tightly justified circumstances.
- Train explicitly on confirmation bias and suggestion effects.
- Separate witnesses and document witness-contact controls.
- Use strategic evidence handling to avoid contaminating first accounts.

### 5) Disclosure management (evidence disclosure strategy)
**Practical recommendations**
- Use written disclosure plans: what evidence is withheld, when it will be disclosed, and why.
- Delay detailed evidence disclosure until free recall and clarification are exhausted.
- Record what was disclosed pre-interview (including to defense counsel).
- Audit for premature disclosure and resulting account contamination risk.

### 6) Documentation and recording standards
**Practical recommendations**
- Make full-session electronic recording the default (rights notice through closure).
- If recording fails/is refused, require written justification and enhanced contemporaneous documentation.
- Capture all persons present, interruptions, breaks, legal consultations, interpretation events, and evidence-presentations.
- Implement retention, chain-of-custody, and tamper-evident controls.

### 7) Digital interview environments (remote/live-link)
**Practical recommendations**
- Add a “remote suitability” screen (age, cognitive status, privacy, coercion risk, tech stability).
- Use secure platforms with access control; verify privacy at both ends on-record.
- Assign neutral facilitators and prohibit high-risk locations (e.g., spaces linked to suspects/coercive actors).
- Define fallback procedures for connection loss, translation failure, or safeguarding concerns.

### 8) Legal safeguards (right to counsel / right to silence)
**Practical recommendations**
- Deliver rights in plain language and verify understanding (teach-back).
- Ensure counsel access before substantive suspect questioning unless lawful emergency exceptions apply.
- Treat invocation of silence/counsel as protected conduct, not a trigger for pressure tactics.
- Record rights advisement, waivers, counsel requests, and timing in auditable form.

### 9) Ethics and human rights concerns
**Practical recommendations**
- Codify non-coercive interviewing as policy doctrine (truth-seeking, not confession-seeking).
- Build independent oversight, complaint pathways, and remediation mechanisms.
- Require vulnerability assessment and reasonable adjustments as standard practice.
- Integrate anti-torture safeguards and dignity protections into SOPs and supervisor review.

### 10) Interviewer training, QA, and supervision
**Practical recommendations**
- Use competency-based certification and recertification, not one-time classroom completion.
- Require routine file/video review with structured feedback from trained supervisors/peers.
- Monitor skill drift post-training and prescribe booster coaching.
- Combine technical QA with interviewer wellbeing supports (burnout/secondary trauma mitigation).

### 11) Metrics for interview quality (agency dashboard)
**Practical recommendations**
- Track technique quality: proportion open prompts/invitations, leading question rate, interruption rate, disclosure timing fidelity.
- Track safeguards: rights advisement completeness, counsel access timing, interpreter compliance, full-recording rate.
- Track outcomes: evidential yield, statement-evidence consistency indicators, suppression/complaint rates, re-interview frequency.
- Track workforce quality: certification status, QA review completion, drift indicators, remediation completion.

## Cross-cutting implementation sequence (agency level)
1. Publish a non-coercive interviewing policy standard aligned to legal safeguards and vulnerability assessment.  
2. Standardize protocols (adult suspect, victim/witness, child/vulnerable, interpreter-mediated, remote).  
3. Build recording + QA infrastructure and supervisor calibration cycles.  
4. Launch metrics dashboard and quarterly governance review tied to corrective actions.
