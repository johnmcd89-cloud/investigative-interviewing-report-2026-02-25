Risk of false/inaccurate admissions is probably lower in non-custodial workplace investigations than in criminal custodial interrogations, but it is not low enough to ignore.  
Inference note: this “lower but non-trivial” conclusion is inferred from adjacent evidence, because direct prevalence studies in corporate/internal investigations are limited.

**What evidence shows**
1. Criminal interrogation research shows strong false-confession risk under accusatory methods.
- Accusatorial approaches produce materially more false confessions than information-gathering approaches (2024 Campbell update/meta-analysis).  
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11465838/
- Classic findings: minimization/leniency cues increase both true and false confessions.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15943675/
- Risk factors include youth/cognitive vulnerability, long questioning, false-evidence tactics, minimization.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19603261/
- Wrongful-conviction datasets show confessions are a real contributor (for example, 29% in Innocence Project DNA exonerations; registry data also tracks false confessions).  
  https://innocenceproject.org/dna-exonerations-in-the-united-states/  
  https://exonerationregistry.org/false-confessions

2. Non-custodial/workplace settings still have pressure channels that can distort admissions.
- Retaliation/job-loss economics matter: when expected retaliation cost falls (higher UI protection), employee reporting rises and more violations are detected (natural-experiment evidence).  
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2020.101385
- Fair process increases internal reporting intent (organizational justice evidence).  
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aos.2010.09.002
- Authority climate and psychological safety strongly influence whether employees speak up.  
  https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.26279183
- Meta-analytic evidence links silence to leader/context factors and downstream harm.  
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-021-09788-7
- Power distance/fear of authority impairs communication upward.  
  https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/19/5/2932

3. Interview credibility judgment is fragile if based on “gut feel.”
- Humans detect deception only slightly above chance on average (~54%).  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/
- Confidence in lie judgments is weakly related to actual accuracy.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15661668/
- Demeanor-heavy approaches are weak; verbal/account content and interview design matter more.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21707129/  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17211691/

4. Language/culture/interpreter effects can degrade evidence quality.
- Miscommunication is common in investigative interviews and can affect outcomes.  
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11896-022-09495-w
- Interpreter-mediated interviews introduce accuracy/control challenges without strong protocol.  
  https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/64760a63-52fa-4e0e-a8e6-7e83bcfbefb9  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35152056/

**Practical framework for low-corroboration workplace cases**
1. Use an information-gathering model, not accusatory.
- Start with open narrative, then probe specifics, then challenge inconsistencies with evidence.
- Avoid implied leniency threats/promises.

2. Pre-commit to competing hypotheses.
- H1 alleged misconduct occurred.
- H2 misunderstanding/error/no misconduct.
- H3 partial misconduct with alternative mechanism.
- Require explicit disconfirming checks for each hypothesis before conclusion.

3. Structured credibility scoring (not demeanor-led).
- Rate each account on: plausibility, internal consistency, external consistency, motive to falsify, opportunity to observe, corroboration quality.
- Treat demeanor as low-weight only (research basis above).
- Keep a written “why” for each score.

4. Evidence quality ladder.
- Tier A: contemporaneous digital/physical records.
- Tier B: independent witness accounts close in time.
- Tier C: delayed recollections/hearsay.
- Don’t let multiple low-independence items masquerade as strong corroboration.

5. Decision categories with confidence calibration.
- `Substantiated` (high confidence): convergent evidence, key alternatives tested.
- `Inconclusive` (moderate/low confidence): unresolved material contradiction.
- `Unsubstantiated` (moderate confidence): evidence favors non-occurrence but uncertainty remains.
- Force numeric confidence bands (for example: 0.55–0.70, 0.70–0.85, >0.85) and record rationale.

6. Mandatory second review triggers.
- Single-source core allegation.
- High authority differential (manager vs junior).
- Interpreter/language complexity.
- High sanction consequence (termination, regulatory exposure).
- Major reliance on credibility assessment over hard evidence.

7. Documentation standard (defensibility).
- Investigation scope, allegations, and time window.
- Interview plan and order.
- Exact evidence list with provenance/chain.
- Credibility matrix and hypothesis tests performed.
- What was not available and why.
- Confidence rating and uncertainty statement.
- Final determination and proportional actions.
- Record retention and audit trail.

**Regulatory/process anchors (workplace context)**
- EEOC (U.S.) requires prompt, adequate, impartial investigation; preserving records; trained investigator (current harassment guidance, April 29, 2024).  
  https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-harassment-workplace  
  https://www.eeoc.gov/summary-key-provisions-eeoc-enforcement-guidance-harassment-workplace
- EEOC legacy guidance gives explicit credibility factors (plausibility, motive, corroboration, etc.) that remain practically useful even though superseded.  
  https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-vicarious-liability-unlawful-harassment-supervisors
- ACAS (UK; reviewed November 4, 2025) provides operational fairness steps for workplace investigations and witness statements.  
  https://www.acas.org.uk/acas-guide-to-conducting-workplace-investigations  
  https://www.acas.org.uk/investigations-for-discipline-and-grievance-step-by-step/step-4-holding-investigation-meetings/step-4-if-there-are-witnesses
- DOJ ECCP (updated September 2024) emphasizes risk-based, tested, documented compliance/investigation systems.  
  https://www.justice.gov/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/download
