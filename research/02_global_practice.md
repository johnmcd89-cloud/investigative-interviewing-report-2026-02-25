Comparative brief (source-linked) on investigative interviewing by region:

1. **UK (England/Wales)**
- **Framework:** PEACE, explicitly embedded in College of Policing investigative interviewing guidance/courses (information-gathering orientation).
- **Training:** College pathways include interviewer and interview manager tracks.
- **Recording:** PACE **Code E** (audio) and **Code F** (video) govern suspect interview recording.
- **Rights caution:** PACE **Code C** includes the standard caution and right to legal advice.
- **Juvenile/vulnerable protections:** Code C “appropriate adult” safeguards; ABE guidance for vulnerable/intimidated witnesses; special measures regime.
- **Major reform:** 2023 updates to PACE codes (incl. revised C/D/H and new I).

2. **US (federal vs state/local)**
- **Framework:** Mixed. Federal practice shows stronger shift toward research-led, rapport/information-gathering (FBI/HIG), while state/local practice remains fragmented and may still include accusatorial vendor methods (e.g., Reid).  
  - **Inference:** “Mixed” is inferred from coexistence of HIG/FLETC material and state/local legal/training variance.
- **Training:** FBI/HIG develops research + practitioner training; FLETC runs federal interviewing courses.
- **Recording:** DOJ policy (Justice Manual) presumes recording of custodial interviews by major DOJ investigative agencies in federal cases.
- **Rights caution:** Miranda baseline; juvenile interrogation doctrine/reforms continue to evolve.
- **State/local differences (examples):** WA broad recording statute; IL and NC statute examples; CA youth counsel-before-waiver rule.
- **Major reforms:** DOJ electronic-recording policy era; multiple state reforms expanding recording/youth protections.

3. **Canada**
- **Framework:** Publicly visible national training offerings are largely PEACE-aligned/information-gathering.
- **Training:** Canadian Police Knowledge Network/CPC investigator interviewing program references PEACE.
- **Rights caution:** Charter s.10(b) right to counsel without delay.
- **Juvenile protections:** YCJA s.146 sets strict admissibility conditions for youth statements (rights explanation + access to counsel/adult support).
- **Agency examples:** RCMP operates under Charter/YCJA framework; public detailed RCMP interview doctrine is less centralized than UK/AFP publications.
- **Sources:**  
  - https://cpkn.ca/course/interviewing-and-interrogation-for-criminal-investigators-program/  
  - https://www.justice.gc.ca/eng/csj-sjc/rfc-dlc/ccrf-ccdl/check/art10b.html

  - https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2000/2000scc38/2000scc38.html

4. **Australia / New Zealand**
- **Australia framework:** AFP publicly adopted PEACE model (2023), explicitly citing rapport-led interviewing.
- **Australia recording/rights:** Commonwealth Crimes Act Part IC includes interview-rights architecture and recording provisions (incl. confessions).
- **NZ framework:** IPCA review indicates NZ Police moved from PEACE to CIPEM (2017 model change).
- **NZ rights caution:** NZ Bill of Rights Act s.23 covers arrest/detention rights (incl. lawyer access).
- **Sources:**

5. **Norway / Scandinavia**
- **Framework:** Norway is a leading investigative-interviewing jurisdiction (KREATIV lineage), emphasizing information-gathering and strategic evidence use over confession-pressure tactics.
- **Training/reform:** Long-running shift documented in Scandinavian/peer-reviewed literature; often cited internationally as reform model.
- **Inference:** Practical implementation details by agency are better documented in research literature than in easily accessible English statutory pages.
- **Sources:**  
  - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359178908000704  
  - https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2578983X.2023.2218928

6. **Netherlands / Germany / France**
- **Netherlands:** Government suspect-rights guidance reflects counsel/rights-based interview structure.
- **Germany:** StPO §136a explicitly prohibits coercive/deceptive/abusive interrogation methods.
- **France:** CPP provisions define garde-à-vue rights notifications and lawyer participation; audiovisual recording mandated in specified serious-crime contexts.
- **Inference:** Compared to Anglo PEACE branding, these systems are more legal-code-driven and judicially structured than model-branded.
- **Sources:**

7. **Selected Asia / Middle East / Africa / Latin America examples**
- **Asia (Japan):** Reforms introduced mandatory recording in selected case categories (not universal), with ongoing debate about scope/effect.
- **Middle East (Iraq example):** EUAM-Iraq reports PEACE-model interview training support to Iraqi institutions.
- **Africa (South Africa):** Constitution (s.35) establishes interview-relevant rights (silence, counsel, fair process safeguards).
- **Latin America (Chile):** Law 21.057 created video-recorded investigative interview system for child victims/witnesses using trained interviewers.
- **Sources:**

**Bottom line trend:** Most reforming jurisdictions are moving toward **recorded, rights-heavy, rapport/information-gathering interviewing**, with strongest formalization in UK/PEACE-derived systems and increasingly in Australia/Nordic practice; the US remains the most **federated/mixed**.
