**Internal Investigation Interview Best Practices (Major Tech Context)**  
`Current-status note`: The EEOC rescinded its 2024 harassment guidance on **January 23, 2026**; use active EEOC resources plus other authorities below. Source: [EEOC rescission notice](https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/eeoc-commission-votes-rescind-2024-harassment-guidance)

1. **Interview planning and scope**
- Define mandate, issues, and legal basis before interviews; document scope changes. Source: [DOJ ECCP (Sept. 2024)](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl?inline=)
- Route allegations through a trusted, well-resourced intake channel with anti-retaliation protections and timely triage. Sources: [DOJ ECCP](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl?inline=), [EEOC Promising Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/promising-practices-preventing-harassment)
- Plan witness order deliberately (complainant, key witnesses, respondent, follow-ups), and define interim risk controls up front (access limits, reporting lines, monitoring). Source: [EEOC Promising Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/promising-practices-preventing-harassment)

2. **Trauma-informed + anti-retaliation posture**
- Use trauma-informed conditions: psychological safety, transparency, choice/control where possible, and retraumatization avoidance. Source: [SAMHSA trauma-informed care](https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/trauma-violence/trauma-informed-care)
- Use open-ended, non-leading prompts, allow pauses/breaks, and avoid demeanor-only credibility judgments. Source: [NIJ practical guide](https://nij.ojp.gov/topics/articles/practical-guide-interviewing-potential-human-trafficking-victims)
- Give clear anti-retaliation reminders to all participants and actively monitor for retaliation after interviews. Sources: [EEOC retaliation](https://www.eeoc.gov/retaliation), [DOJ ECCP](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl?inline=)
- Do not use confidentiality language that could impede protected reporting to regulators. Sources: [SEC whistleblower protections](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/whistleblower-program/whistleblower-protections), [SEC Rule 21F-17 enforcement example](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2015-54)

3. **Role clarity (not law enforcement) + Upjohn warnings**
- State role clearly: this is a company fact-finding interview, not a police interrogation. Source: [NLRB Weingarten page (Miranda comparison)](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/your-rights/weingarten-rights)
- Where counsel is conducting interviews, give a clear Upjohn warning: counsel represents the company, privilege belongs to company, and company may waive privilege. Sources: [Upjohn v. U.S.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/449/383), [ABA practical guidance](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2023-winter/contractor-compliance-internal-investigations-practical-strategies/)
- Avoid implying personal representation or neutrality as to attorney role; this is a common privilege/ethics failure point. Sources: [ABA privilege pitfalls](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/commercial-business/four-strategies-avoiding-privilege-pitfalls/), [WilmerHale privilege alert](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/preserving-the-corporate-privilege-in-internal-investigations-dc-circuit-clarifies-scope-of-the-privilege-in-important-series-of-decisions)

4. **Representation rights (jurisdiction-dependent)**
- **U.S. (NLRA):** under current Board law, Weingarten interview-representation rights apply to union-represented employees who request representation in qualifying investigatory interviews. Source: [NLRB Weingarten rights](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/your-rights/weingarten-rights)
- **UK:** statutory accompaniment generally applies to disciplinary/grievance hearings; disciplinary investigation meetings generally have no statutory accompaniment right (but allowing a companion is often good practice). Sources: [Acas Code](https://www.acas.org.uk/acas-code-of-practice-on-disciplinary-and-grievance-procedures/html), [Acas Step 4 investigations](https://www.acas.org.uk/investigations-for-discipline-and-grievance-step-by-step/step-4-holding-investigation-meetings)
- **Inference from sources:** for multinational tech employers, maintain a country-by-country representation-rights matrix approved by local counsel before interviews.

5. **Documentation standards**
- Keep contemporaneous records for intake, scope decisions, interview logistics, evidence reviewed, credibility rationale, findings, and remediation tracking. Sources: [DOJ ECCP](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl?inline=), [EEOC Promising Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/promising-practices-preventing-harassment)
- Separate factual record from legal analysis in workpapers; control distribution on need-to-know basis to reduce privilege waiver risk. Sources: [Upjohn](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/449/383), [ABA privilege guidance](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/commercial-business/four-strategies-avoiding-privilege-pitfalls/)

6. **Fairness procedures**
- Ensure promptness, impartiality, neutrality, and equal opportunity to respond; avoid premature guilt assumptions. Sources: [EEOC Promising Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/promising-practices-preventing-harassment), [Acas Code](https://www.acas.org.uk/acas-code-of-practice-on-disciplinary-and-grievance-procedures/html)
- Use consistent standards across levels/geographies and provide appeal/review paths for formal outcomes where required. Source: [Acas Code](https://www.acas.org.uk/acas-code-of-practice-on-disciplinary-and-grievance-procedures/html)

7. **Legal/HR/security coordination**
- Define ownership early: Legal (privilege/regulatory), HR/ER (policy/process/fairness), Security/IT (evidence integrity), Compliance (remediation/root-cause). Source: [DOJ ECCP](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl?inline=)
- Keep internal investigations independent and not directed by prosecutors when pursuing cooperation credit. Source: [Skadden analysis](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2023/09/quarterly-insights/investigations-for-self-disclosure)

8. **Quality controls and continuous improvement**
- Measure cycle time, substantiation patterns, consistency of consequences, repeat-issue rates, and retaliation incidents. Source: [DOJ ECCP](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl?inline=)
- Periodically test hotline/reporting channels and audit investigation quality. Sources: [DOJ ECCP](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl?inline=), [EEOC Promising Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/promising-practices-preventing-harassment)
- Anchor program design to recognized compliance frameworks. Sources: [ISO 37002](https://www.iso.org/standard/65035.html), [ISO 37301](https://www.iso.org/standard/75080.html), [U.S.S.G. Chapter 8](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2025-guidelines-manual/annotated-2025-chapter-8)
- **Inference from peer-reviewed evidence:** information-gathering, rapport-based interviewing improves information quality and lowers error risk versus stress-heavy or accusatory styles. Sources: [Stress-memory meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15732653/), [Cognitive Interview field test](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2793772/), [Rapport/question-type meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33454643/)
